Infinity

Two new questions: what happens when $x$ runs off forever, and what happens when the bottom of a fraction shrinks to zero. Both come down to one habit: ask which part is biggest.

Running off to the right

Take $\frac{1}{x}$ and feed it bigger and bigger $x$: $\frac{1}{10} = 0.1$, $\frac{1}{1000} = 0.001$, $\frac{1}{1\,000\,000} = 0.000001$. The values crowd toward 0 and stay there. We write

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x} = 0.$$

Read “$x \to \infty$” as “as $x$ grows without bound”. $\infty$ isn’t a number you plug in; it’s a direction you walk. Same idea as before: the limit is the height the function settles toward.

A fixed number divided by something huge is nearly 0: $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{c}{x} = 0$, and the same for $\frac{c}{x^2}$, $\frac{c}{x^3}$, …

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(2 + \frac{5}{x}\right)$

$\frac{5}{x} \to 0$ as $x$ grows, so the whole thing heads to $2 + 0 = 2$. On a graph, the curve hugs the horizontal line $y = 2$: a horizontal asymptote.

Shrinking the bottom: blow-ups

Lesson 2’s rule said nonzero over 0 means the function blows up. Lesson 3 said sides matter. Put them together with

$$f(x) = \frac{x+1}{x-2}.$$

At $x = 2$ the top is $3$ and the bottom is $0$. Drag $x$ closer to 2 and watch each side.

from the left, $f(2-d)$
from the right, $f(2+d)$

Why the opposite signs? Near 2 the top is about $3$ (positive). The bottom $x - 2$ is a tiny number whose sign depends on the side:

  • Right of 2, e.g. $x = 2.001$: bottom $= +0.001$, so $\frac{3}{0.001} = 3000$. Heads to $+\infty$.
  • Left of 2, e.g. $x = 1.999$: bottom $= -0.001$, so $\frac{3}{-0.001} = -3000$. Heads to $-\infty$.

The vertical line $x = 2$ that the graph shoots up and down beside is a vertical asymptote.

Sign recipe for a blow-up: find the sign of the top near $a$, find the sign of the tiny bottom on the side you care about, multiply the signs.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2^-}\frac{x+1}{x-2}$ is…

Top near 3: positive. Bottom just left of 2: tiny and negative. Positive over tiny negative is a huge negative, so $-\infty$.

Careful, this one is not a blow-up: $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 3}\frac{x-3}{(x-3)(x+1)}$. (Decimal is fine.)

Plugging in gives $\frac{0}{0}$, not nonzero over 0, so simplify first.

Cancel $(x - 3)$ (fine, since $x \ne 3$ inside a limit): $\frac{1}{x+1}$.

Plug in: $\frac{1}{3+1} = \frac14 = 0.25$. Only a nonzero top over a zero bottom gives an asymptote; $\frac00$ often hides a plain hole.

Dominant terms

Now a fraction running off to infinity:

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^2 + 5x}{x^2 + 1}$$

Top and bottom both grow without bound, so “$\frac{\infty}{\infty}$” tells you nothing yet (like $\frac00$). The question is which terms matter. Slide $x$ up and watch how much of each line is made of the biggest term.

At $x = 1$ the $5x$ is most of the top. By $x = 1000$ it’s a rounding error: the top is basically $3x^2$, the bottom basically $x^2$, and the fraction is basically $\frac{3x^2}{x^2} = 3$.

Making it exact: divide by the top power

“Basically” becomes algebra with one move: divide every term, top and bottom, by the highest power of $x$ in the bottom (here $x^2$). Dividing top and bottom by the same thing doesn’t change the fraction. Tap through:

  1. Divide every term by $x^2$: $$\frac{3x^2 + 5x}{x^2 + 1} = \frac{\frac{3x^2}{x^2} + \frac{5x}{x^2}}{\frac{x^2}{x^2} + \frac{1}{x^2}}$$

  2. Simplify each piece: $\frac{3x^2}{x^2} = 3$, $\frac{5x}{x^2} = \frac{5}{x}$, $\frac{x^2}{x^2} = 1$. So $$\frac{3 + \frac5x}{1 + \frac{1}{x^2}}$$

  3. As $x \to \infty$: $\frac5x \to 0$ and $\frac{1}{x^2} \to 0$ (the key fact from the start).

  4. What’s left: $\frac{3 + 0}{1 + 0} = 3$.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{4x^3 - x}{2x^3 + 7}$

Divide every term by $x^3$: $\frac{4 - \frac{1}{x^2}}{2 + \frac{7}{x^3}}$.

The small pieces go to 0: $\frac{4 - 0}{2 + 0} = \frac42 = 2$. Shortcut: same top power on both sides, so the answer is the ratio of the leading numbers, $\frac42$.

The three cases

Compare the highest power on top with the highest power on the bottom:

  • Bottom wins (e.g. $\frac{5x + 2}{x^2 - 1}$): divide by $x^2$ to get $\frac{\frac5x + \frac{2}{x^2}}{1 - \frac{1}{x^2}} \to \frac{0}{1} = 0$.
  • Tie (e.g. $\frac{3x^2 + 5x}{x^2+1}$): the limit is the ratio of the leading numbers, $\frac31 = 3$.
  • Top wins (e.g. $\frac{x^2 + 1}{x + 3}$): divide by $x$ to get $\frac{x + \frac1x}{1 + \frac3x}$. The top still has a bare $x$ that keeps growing, so the fraction grows without bound: no finite limit.

As $x \to \infty$, only the highest power on top and the highest power on the bottom matter. Everything else fades to nothing next to them.

In your own words: why is it safe to ignore the $+5x$ in $3x^2 + 5x$ when $x$ is huge, but not when $x$ is near 0?

Their ratio is $\frac{5x}{3x^2} = \frac{5}{3x}$. For huge $x$ that’s tiny, so $5x$ is a vanishing fraction of $3x^2$. Near 0 it’s the reverse: $\frac{5}{3x}$ is huge, so $5x$ is the bigger term. “Dominant” depends on where you’re looking.

Check

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5x+2}{x^2-1}$ is…

The bottom’s power ($x^2$) beats the top’s ($x$). Divide by $x^2$: $\frac{\frac5x + \frac{2}{x^2}}{1 - \frac{1}{x^2}} \to \frac{0 + 0}{1 - 0} = 0$.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1 - 6x}{3x + 4}$ (mind the sign)

Highest power is $x$ on both sides. Divide every term by $x$: $\frac{\frac1x - 6}{3 + \frac4x}$.

As $x \to \infty$: $\frac{0 - 6}{3 + 0} = \frac{-6}{3} = -2$. The leading term on top is $-6x$, and its minus sign comes along.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -1^+}\frac{2}{x+1}$ is…

Top: 2, positive. Just right of $-1$, e.g. $x = -0.999$, the bottom is $-0.999 + 1 = 0.001$: tiny and positive. Positive over tiny positive is huge and positive, so $+\infty$.

Which has a finite limit as $x \to \infty$?

Only the middle one ties on power ($x^2$ over $x^2$), giving $\frac72$. The other two have a bigger power on top, so they grow without bound.
