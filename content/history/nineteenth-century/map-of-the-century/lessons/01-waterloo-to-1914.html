From Waterloo to 1914 in fifteen minutes

Before zooming in on 1873, get the whole century in your head: four forces that drove it, and the hinge in the middle where the mood turned.

Why start with a map

Liaquat Ahamed’s 1873 (2026) makes its claim in its subtitle: The Rothschilds, the First Great Depression, and the Making of the Modern World. A financial crash, a long slump, and a banking family, put forward as the hinge of an era.

To judge a claim like that you need to know what came before and after. Historians often call the period from the French Revolution to the First World War the long nineteenth century (Eric Hobsbawm’s phrase). We’ll start a little later, at Waterloo in 1815, when Europe finally stopped fighting Napoleon.

Four engines

Almost everything in the century runs on one of these four, and they keep feeding each other.

IndustrySteam power, railways, factories, cities. Output and speed multiply; Britain goes first and Germany and the United States catch up fast.
MoneyThe gold standard, London as the world’s banker, bond markets that finance railways and governments everywhere, and dynasties like the Rothschilds at the centre.
NationsNationalism and mass politics. Italy and Germany are forged by war; the United States nearly splits and is rebuilt.
EmpireSteamships, telegraphs and the Suez Canal shrink the world. Trade and migration boom, then a rush to grab colonies.

A German bank lends to an American railroad, paid back in gold-backed money. Which two engines is that mostly?

Cross-border capital (money) financing railways (industry). This exact pairing is what blew up in 1873: railroad bonds sold across the Atlantic, financed on credit.

The timeline

Filter by engine to see each thread. The filled dots mark the hinge years around 1873.

    What did Britain’s repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846 do?

    The Corn Laws protected British landowners by taxing imported grain. Repeal meant cheaper bread for industrial workers and signalled that Britain, the workshop of the world, would trade freely. Free trade spread from there, peaking with treaties like Cobden–Chevalier (1860).

    Two halves

    One classic way to read the century is as two halves split in the 1870s. Hobsbawm’s trilogy draws the line there too: The Age of Capital ends in 1875, and The Age of Empire picks up from it.

    Before ~1873

    Growth, railway booms, free trade spreading, confidence in markets and progress. Britain dominant. Money and goods move more freely than ever before.

    After ~1873

    Two decades of falling prices and squeezed profits. Tariffs return (Germany 1879), mass politics and socialism grow, and the great powers race for colonies. Rivalries harden toward 1914.

    The 1873 crash sits at the seam between an age of liberal optimism and an age of protection, empire and rivalry. Whether it caused the turn or merely marked it is the question to carry into the rest of this topic.

    Check

    In 1873 a crash came in two waves. Which order is right?

    The Vienna stock exchange collapsed in May 1873 after a speculative boom. In September the American bank Jay Cooke & Company, overextended on railroad bonds, failed, and the New York Stock Exchange shut for days. Lesson 4 of the 1873 topic goes through both.

    What best describes the “Long Depression” that followed?

    It was mainly deflation. Prices fell for over two decades, which hurt debtors, farmers and profit margins, even as output in many countries kept growing. Historians still argue about whether “depression” is the right word.

    Which came first?

    Suez in 1869, the German Empire in January 1871 (proclaimed at Versailles, of all places), the Berlin Conference in 1884–85.

    In two sentences: why might a long slump push governments toward tariffs and colonies?

    When prices and profits fall for years, farmers and manufacturers demand protection from cheap imports, and politicians facing newly enfranchised voters listen. Colonies promised protected markets, raw materials and prestige at a time when open trade looked like it was hurting rather than helping.
