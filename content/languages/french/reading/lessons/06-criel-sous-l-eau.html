Actualité : le pétrolier d’Ibiza

On the night of 25 November a tanker carrying diesel capsized off Ibiza. It set off, took on water, turned over, and its crew were rescued. That makes it a good test of avoir or être.

Source: franceinfo, 26–27 novembre 2026. Lire l’article original

Warm-up

Last lesson's three questions: avoir or être? which form? does it agree?

The slip that came up most last time was a tombé. Tomber is always être in the past: il est tombé, never il a tombé. And when the subject is a group of men, or men and women mixed, the participle takes just -s, not -es.

Un arbre ______ sur la route. (tomber)

est tombé. rester ↔ tomber is on the list, so être. Un arbre is masculine singular, so the participle stays bare: tombé.

Les secours ______ très vite. (venir; a mixed team)

sont venus. aller ↔ venir, so être. A mixed group counts as masculine plural: -s only. -es is only for a group that is all women.

L’eau ______ dans le bateau. (entrer)

est entrée. entrer ↔ sortir is on the list. L’eau is feminine, so the participle takes -e.

Tu ______ à quelle heure cette nuit ? (se réveiller)

t’es réveillé: subject · pronoun · être · participle. After tu it's es; te réveilles is the present.

Before you read

  • Ibiza: one of Spain's Balearic Islands (les Baléares), in the Mediterranean, east of Valencia
  • un pétrolier: a tanker (a ship that carries oil or fuel) · un navire: a ship, the newsroom word for un bateau
  • l’équipage (m): the crew · un marin: a sailor
  • chavirer: to capsize, to turn over · couler: to sink
  • au large de: off (the coast of), out at sea near

This version is another step closer to real news: more navire than bateau, a few passives (ont été secourus), selon, and a sentence in the future. Watch the verbs of movement: some take être, and some, like chavirer, don't.

It left Algeciras, in the south of Spain, on 24 November, with 17 sailors on board.

It was carrying up to 7,000 tonnes of diesel and was due in Barcelona on the Thursday morning.

But on the Wednesday evening, for a reason still unknown, water got into the ship.

Around 10 p.m. the ship suddenly slowed down, and a distress call was sent out.

During the night it capsized, about 50 kilometres off the coast of Ibiza.

All 17 crew members, every one of them Turkish or Indian, were rescued.

One of them, the chief engineer, a 46-year-old Turkish man, died in hospital.

Jeudi midi, le navire était quille en l’air, mais il n’a pas coulé : il est resté à la surface.

At midday on Thursday the ship was upside down, but it hadn't sunk: it stayed on the surface.

The Spanish government launched its national emergency plan. The authorities went straight to level 2, skipping levels 0 and 1.

According to an expert at the University of Cádiz, that's because the area is especially vulnerable.

It is rich in Posidonia meadows, a plant that grows on the seabed.

The reports give different distances from Ibiza; “about 50 km” sits between them.

Where was the Med Emre going?

Il est parti d’Algésiras … et devait arriver à Barcelone. Cartagena is where it's being towed now, not where it was heading.

What does il n’a pas coulé : il est resté à la surface tell you about the ship?

Couler is to sink, so il n’a pas coulé means it didn't sink. It did capsize (il a chaviré; quille en l’air, keel up), but it stayed on the surface. The first headlines said it sank; later reports corrected that.

What happened to the crew?

Les 17 membres de l’équipage … ont été secourus. Seven went to hospital, and the chief engineer est mort à l’hôpital.

Why did Spain go straight to level 2 of its emergency plan?

C’est parce que la zone est particulièrement vulnérable: the Posidonia meadows. No pollution had been found at that point: aucune trace de pollution.

Why tow the ship to Cartagena?

Dans une eau calme, on pourra enfin décharger le carburant. Notice sera remorqué and pourra: the future, which is Lesson 8.

Now try the original

franceinfo's “what we know” piece is denser than last week's France 3 story, with more maritime and official vocabulary. These are words it uses that I swapped for simpler ones:

  • battant pavillon maltais: flying the Maltese flag. Le pavillon is a ship's flag, which tells you where it's registered. I just wrote maltais.
  • a subi une voie d’eau: sprang a leak. Une voie d’eau is a leak in a hull; I wrote l’eau est entrée.
  • a brusquement viré de bord: suddenly changed course. Virer is to turn.
  • l’armateur: the shipowner (here, a Turkish company)
  • l’homme qui a succombé: the man who died. Succomber is the formal way to die of injuries.
  • une vedette de sauvetage: a rescue launch, a fast rescue boat. Une vedette is also a star (a film star).
  • à titre préventif: as a precaution
  • la protection du littoral: protecting the coastline. Le littoral is the coast, the shore.

Not every movement verb is on the list

The article is full of movement, but only some of it takes être. Il est parti, l’eau est entrée, il est mort, il est resté: all on the list. But il a ralenti, il a chaviré, il n’a pas coulé. Slowing, capsizing and sinking feel like movement, but they're not on the list, so they take avoir.

Don't decide by meaning. Check the list. Mourir, naître and rester are on it; chavirer, couler, ralentir, courir and marcher aren't.

Le bateau ______ pendant la nuit. (chavirer)

a chaviré. Chavirer isn't on the être list, so it takes avoir, and with avoir there's no agreement.

Le chef mécanicien ______ à l’hôpital.

est mort. naître ↔ mourir is on the list, so être, and the participle is irregular: mort (feminine morte).

Same verb, two auxiliaries

The article says les autorités sont passées directement au niveau 2: passer with être, agreeing with les autorités. But you'll also meet il a passé la nuit à l’hôpital. Same verb, different auxiliary. What changed?

In the second sentence passer has a direct object: what was passed (spent) is la nuit. A handful of verbs from the être list do this. With no object they take être; with an object they switch to avoir and stop agreeing.

passer

Elles sont passées au niveau 2.

Il a passé toute la nuit à attendre.

sortir

Il est sorti de la cabine.

Il a sorti son gilet de sauvetage.

monter

L’eau est montée très vite.

Ils ont monté les valises à bord.

descendre

Elle est descendue du bateau.

Elle a descendu les bouteilles à la cave.

passer, sortir, monter, descendre, rentrer, retourner: no object → être. A direct object right after the verb → avoir. (De la cabine, au niveau 2, très vite are places or manners, not objects.)

One more avoir worth knowing: être's own past is a été, and the article's passives are built on it. Les marins ont été secourus. Une alerte a été lancée. Never sont été. The agreement goes on the last participle: secourus, lancée.

Nous ______ la nuit à attendre des nouvelles.

avons passé. La nuit is the thing being passed (spent), a direct object, so avoir and no agreement.

Samedi, Mme Leclerc ______ chez elle. (rentrer)

est rentrée. No object (chez elle is where she went), so être; feminine, so -e.

Il ______ ses cartons à l’étage. (monter)

a monté. There's an object, ses cartons (his boxes), so avoir, and with avoir, no agreement.

Dans la cale, l’eau ______ très vite. (monter)

est montée. No object this time (très vite says how), so être; l’eau is feminine, so -e. (La cale is a ship's hold.)

Les passagers ______ sur le pont. (sortir)

sont sortis. No object (sur le pont, on deck, is a place), so être; masculine (or mixed) plural, so -s.

Ils ______ les gilets de sauvetage. (sortir)

ont sorti. Les gilets is a direct object, so avoir, and the participle doesn't agree: sorti, not sortis.

Elle ______ tout de suite. (ne pas s’inquiéter, passé composé)

ne s’est pas inquiétée. Reflexive, so être and agreement (-e for elle). The ne … pas wraps the pronoun and the auxiliary together: ne s’est pas, then the participle.

Check

Mes parents ______ trois jours à Ibiza. (rester)

sont restés. Rester is always être; mes parents is a mixed plural, so -s. (Trois jours is how long, not an object.)

Les 17 marins ______ secourus.

ont été. Être's own past always uses avoir, and été never changes. The agreement goes on secourus, which matches les marins.

Le marin ______ le chien de la cabine. (sortir)

a sorti. Le chien is the thing being taken out, a direct object, so avoir.

Translate: The ship left on Tuesday, and on Wednesday the water came in.

Le navire est parti mardi, et mercredi l’eau est entrée. Both have no object, so both take être. Le navire is masculine, so parti stays bare; l’eau is feminine, so entrée takes -e. Le bateau works too.
